# FCC fill phases and driven response

This is an isolated [review experiment](../../fcc_bunch_sampling.py), not an
addition to the default teaching examples or a packed multibunch implementation.
It uses the same pinned thick Z model as the
[FCC scaling review](../../../designs/2026-11-20_fcc_scaling_review.md).

The six reference centroids occupy integer RF buckets in two illustrative
trains, with empty buckets between them and at the end of the turn. The RF
frequency is about 600.113 MHz and inferred harmonic 181,440; the input's
harmonic/RF revolution period differs from the geometric reference period by
about 19 fs. This is a phase-sampling demonstration, not an FCC engineering fill.

One expression-defined quadrupole, `qf2r.0`, moves horizontally with 100 nm
amplitude at 10 Hz or 1 kHz. Every bunch samples the **same prescribed motion**.
Its whole-line map is frozen at `n*T_rev + arrival_offset`. The runs are
independent and sequential, using `time_origin_s` to retain their relative
phases. Positive arrival delay corresponds to negative bunch-centre zeta in a
packed longitudinal representation. Empty buckets select no samples; they do
not interrupt or reseed the ground field.

This deliberately follows the proposed per-bunch, once-per-turn freeze model.
It omits the additional element passage delay `s/(beta0*c)` and is not a
simultaneous physical survey of all bunch encounters. The current shared scalar
alignment backend cannot apply different mechanical maps to several bunches
packed into one particle array. That needs phase-aware occurrence buffers or
ordered packet scheduling, with an explicit bounded-memory policy.

Two figures separate the relevant observations:

- [Fill and response](fill_and_response.svg): the upper panels show bunch phase
  and actual frozen 4D Twiss closed orbit at the line origin `ipa`. At 10 Hz,
  the last bunch differs by 0.0144 rad from the first; at 1 kHz, by 1.444 rad.
  The lower panels compare tracked motion, a linear driven-map prediction with
  its free transient, and the sequence of frozen closed orbits.
- [Frozen beta beating](frozen_beta_beating.svg): actual 4D Twiss is recomputed
  for every selected bunch phase. It shows optics snapshots, not the response
  of an evolving beam. A horizontal shifted orbit can change the optics through
  sextupole feed-down. A selected phase was checked against halved/doubled
  one-turn-matrix differentiation steps, as detailed below. Profiles are sampled
  every 32 occurrences for plotting. No dynamic Twiss claim follows.

All bunches start on the **same zero-vibration closed orbit**. Longitudinal
coordinates are frozen; radiation/damping, collective effects and feedback are
absent. A free betatron transient therefore remains throughout the 256-turn
record. It must not be mistaken for a failure of the low-frequency approximation.

The independent linear reference uses the zero-error one-turn 4x4 matrix `M`
and the central-difference displacement-to-one-turn-map Jacobian `b`. Its
particular response is `(exp(i*omega*T_rev)*I-M)^-1*b*A`, together with the
homogeneous term required by the common initial condition. The tracked x
records agree with this reference to 0.028% RMS or better. The particular
response amplitude at `ipa`, relative to the static closed-orbit amplitude,
is approximately 1.00076 at 10 Hz and 1.59684 at 1 kHz. Those are specific to
this drive and observation; they are not universal ground-motion gains.

The 1 kHz drive is below the turn Nyquist frequency, but advances by about
1.900 rad per revolution. Nyquist compliance does not establish that a frozen
closed orbit describes the forced beam response. The measured bunch phases
also show why a single global turn number cannot encode high-frequency fill
effects by itself.

The baseline transverse tunes are approximately 170.167 and 138.200, with
revolution frequency 3,307.50 Hz. Their first fractional betatron aliases are
about 552.35 and 661.50 Hz, inside the proposed below-1-kHz vibration band.
These values follow from the measured tunes and revolution period; no resonant
tracking scan was performed. They are another reason to evaluate forced beam
response rather than infer it from the ratio of vibration to revolution
frequency. Damping, feedback and the full source spectrum determine the actual
response near those frequencies.

The [numerical-step check](twiss_convergence.json), generated by
[`fcc_twiss_convergence.py`](../../fcc_twiss_convergence.py), repeats zero-motion
Twiss exactly and compares the selected 1 kHz bunch phase at half/default/double
`steps_R_matrix`. The full-ring maximum beta beating is about 1,045.203 ppm;
the largest pointwise change of that beating across these steps is below
0.000581 ppm. The observed closed orbit at `ipa` is unchanged at the printed
precision. This establishes that this snapshot signal exceeds the tested
one-turn-matrix differentiation error. The W-propagation step was held fixed;
this is a focused convergence check, not a complete optics-system validation.

[Numeric records](bunch_sampling.json) retain the actual Twiss snapshots,
sampled beta profiles, tracked histories, independent predictions, model/source
hashes and assumptions. Reproduce with the same compiler setup as the other
review probes:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 \
    python examples/mechanics/review/fcc_bunch_sampling.py
```
